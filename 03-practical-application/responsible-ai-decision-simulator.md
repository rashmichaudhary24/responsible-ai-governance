# Responsible AI Decision Simulator

## Overview

The **Responsible AI Decision Simulator** is a scenario-based learning experience designed to help learners practise responsible AI judgement in realistic workplace situations.

Rather than asking learners to recall definitions, the simulation presents decisions where an apparently convenient use of AI can introduce privacy, accountability or accuracy risks.

**Format:** Interactive branching scenario  
**Authoring tool:** Articulate Storyline 360  
**AI-assisted development:** ChatGPT  
**Audience:** Employees and business users working with AI in the workplace

## The learning problem

Responsible AI principles are easy to agree with in theory. The harder question is what someone should actually **do** when they are under time pressure and AI appears to offer a convenient shortcut.

The simulator was designed around that gap between **knowing the principle** and **making the decision**.

## How the simulation works

Learners encounter workplace situations involving AI and choose the action they would take. Each decision produces immediate feedback explaining the risk and the reasoning behind the stronger choice.

The experience deliberately avoids trick questions. The objective is to make the consequences of seemingly small decisions visible.

### Scenario 1 — The Helpful Shortcut

A learner is responding to a customer complaint and wants to use ChatGPT to draft a professional response. The available information includes customer names, account numbers, mobile numbers and transaction history.

The learner must decide whether to:

- paste everything into the AI tool;
- remove identifying information and provide only relevant context;
- upload screenshots from the customer account; or
- ask a colleague to use their account instead.

The scenario reinforces **data minimisation, privacy and accountability**.

### Scenario 2 — The Perfect Presentation

A manager needs a presentation quickly. AI generates a polished 15-slide deck containing impressive statistics and claims, but no sources.

The learner must decide whether to send it immediately, review only part of it, validate the claims, or rely on another AI system for confirmation.

The scenario reinforces **human judgement, validation and the limits of AI-generated confidence**.

## Governance principles embedded in the experience

The simulator translates responsible AI principles into observable workplace behaviour:

| Governance principle | Behaviour reinforced |
|---|---|
| **Privacy & data governance** | Remove identifying and unnecessary sensitive information before using AI |
| **Accountability** | Responsibility remains with the person using AI; it cannot simply be shifted to someone else |
| **Human oversight** | AI output requires human review and judgement |
| **Transparency & accuracy** | Important facts, statistics and claims should be validated |
| **Risk awareness** | A useful output can still create a separate governance or compliance risk |
| **Responsible adoption** | Responsible AI is about using AI thoughtfully, not avoiding AI altogether |

## Learning design approach

The simulation uses **scenario-based learning** rather than a conventional knowledge check.

The learner is placed inside a recognisable workplace situation, makes a decision, sees the consequence and receives targeted feedback. This creates a simple learning loop:

**Situation → Decision → Consequence → Reflection → Better judgement**

This approach is particularly useful for responsible AI because many governance failures are not caused by a lack of awareness of the principle. They occur when people fail to apply the principle at the moment a decision has to be made.

## What the experience demonstrates

This artefact demonstrates the ability to:

- translate responsible AI principles into practical workplace situations;
- design scenario-based learning around judgement rather than recall;
- use branching decisions and consequence-based feedback;
- connect AI governance concepts with employee behaviour;
- use AI-assisted content development while retaining human review and instructional judgement;
- create an interactive learning experience using Articulate Storyline 360.

## Outcome

The simulation ends with a simple message:

> **Responsible AI is not about avoiding AI. It is about using AI thoughtfully, validating outputs, protecting sensitive information, and exercising sound judgment.**

That is the central learning objective of the experience.

## Why this belongs in an AI Governance portfolio

The simulator represents the transition from **principles to practice**.

The RBI FREE-AI framework provides principles and recommendations. This artefact explores what responsible AI can look like at the point where an employee actually has to make a decision.

**Principle → Risk → Decision → Behaviour**

That translation is an important part of responsible AI adoption: governance is not only a policy or a control framework; it also depends on whether people understand how to apply it in their everyday work.
