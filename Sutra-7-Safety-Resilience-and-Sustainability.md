# Sutra 7: Safety, Resilience and Sustainability

## Why does it matter?

AI systems used in financial services must be safe and able to keep working when things go wrong. They should be able to detect problems early, respond to them, recover from failures and learn from incidents.

Resilience is not just about preventing failures. It also means detecting issues quickly, fixing them transparently and learning from them so that the same problems are less likely to happen again.

RBI also wants AI adoption to be sustainable. AI should be used across the financial sector in a way that is inclusive, efficient and mindful of the resources it consumes.

## What would this look like in practice?

RBI translates the Safety, Resilience and Sustainability Sutra into practical measures through:

1. **Recommendation 1 — Financial Sector Data Infrastructure:** Establish high-quality financial-sector data infrastructure to support the development of trustworthy AI models.
2. **Recommendation 3 — Incentives and Funding Support:** Consider funding for **data and compute infrastructure** to support AI adoption and strategic sectoral needs.
3. **Recommendation 4 — Indigenous Financial Sector Specific AI Models:** Develop financial-sector-specific AI models and offer them as a **public good**.
4. **Recommendation 5 — Integrating AI with DPI:** Integrate AI with Digital Public Infrastructure to deliver **inclusive, affordable financial services at scale**.
5. **Recommendation 6 — Adaptive and Enabling Policies:** Ensure that policies and legal frameworks address **AI-specific risks**, and provide guidance for AI adoption and risk mitigation.
6. **Recommendation 7 — Enabling AI-Based Affirmative Action:** Encourage AI-driven innovation that expands financial inclusion while **maintaining basic safeguards**.
7. **Recommendation 8 — AI Liability Framework:** Encourage the use of safety measures such as **incident reporting, audits and red teaming**, with a stronger response to repeated breaches, gross negligence or failure to fix identified problems.
8. **Recommendation 9 — AI Institutional Framework:** Establish mechanisms for **continuous monitoring** of emerging AI opportunities and risks and the evolution of AI technology.
9. **Recommendation 10 — Capacity Building within REs:** Build the skills needed across the workforce to **mitigate AI risks** and support responsible AI adoption.
10. **Recommendation 11 — Capacity Building for Regulators and Supervisors:** Ensure regulators and supervisors understand AI technologies and their **associated risks and ethical considerations**, and keep regulatory and supervisory frameworks aligned with the changing AI landscape.
11. **Recommendation 12 — Framework for Sharing Best Practices:** Share AI use cases, lessons learned, challenges and **sound governance frameworks** to support responsible scaling.
12. **Recommendation 13 — Recognise and Reward Responsible AI Innovation:** Recognise responsible AI innovation that demonstrates **positive social impact** and considers ethical issues from the start.
13. **Recommendation 14 — Board Approved AI Policy:** Require Board-approved AI policies covering **risk appetite, operational safeguards, auditability and the AI model lifecycle**.
14. **Recommendation 15 — Data Lifecycle Governance:** Establish controls for **data collection, access, use, retention and deletion** throughout the AI data lifecycle.
15. **Recommendation 16 — AI System Governance Framework:** Manage AI across its entire lifecycle through **validation, ongoing monitoring, detection of model drift and degradation, and human oversight** for higher-risk systems.
16. **Recommendation 17 — Product Approval Process:** Include **AI-specific risk evaluations** in the approval of AI-enabled products and solutions.
17. **Recommendation 18 — Consumer Protection:** Protect customers through **accessible recourse mechanisms** and education about safe AI use and their rights.
18. **Recommendation 19 — Cybersecurity Measures:** Identify security risks arising from AI and strengthen the **hardware, software and processes** used to protect against them. AI may also be used for dynamic threat detection and response.
19. **Recommendation 20 — Red Teaming:** Conduct structured red teaming throughout the AI lifecycle, with the **frequency and intensity based on the level of risk and potential impact**. Repeat testing when threats or circumstances change.
20. **Recommendation 21 — Business Continuity Plan for AI Systems:** Include **AI model performance degradation** in business continuity plans, establish fallback mechanisms and regularly test them through BCP drills.
21. **Recommendation 22 — AI Incident Reporting and Sectoral Risk Intelligence Framework:** Enable **early detection and timely reporting of AI-related incidents** and use a good-faith approach to encourage disclosure.
22. **Recommendation 23 — AI Inventory within REs and Sector-Wide Repository:** Track AI models, use cases, dependencies and risks within REs, and monitor **concentration risks and systemic vulnerabilities** across the financial sector.
23. **Recommendation 24 — AI Audit Framework:** Conduct **risk-based AI audits** across the AI lifecycle and regularly update the audit framework to address emerging risks and technologies.
24. **Recommendation 26 — AI Toolkit:** Help REs validate and demonstrate compliance with responsible AI principles including **robustness**.

*Note: Recommendation numbers and titles follow the RBI FREE-AI Committee Report. The content under this Sutra is a focused extraction of the relevant part of each recommendation; the mapping of recommendations to the Seven Sutras is my interpretation.*
