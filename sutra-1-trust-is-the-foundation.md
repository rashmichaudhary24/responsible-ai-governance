# Sutra 1: Trust is the Foundation

## Why does it matter?

In financial services, people trust institutions with their money, livelihoods and personal information.

Trust can be damaged when AI decisions are difficult to understand, produce unexpected results, use data inappropriately, or introduce problems such as bias, hallucinations, privacy breaches or security risks. Unclear responsibility can make matters worse. Loss of trust can lead to customer harm, fraud, legal action, reputational damage and, in extreme cases, wider problems for the financial system.

AI should therefore strengthen that trust, not create new reasons to lose it.

## What would this look like in practice?

RBI translates the Trust Sutra into practical measures through the following recommendations:

1. **Recommendation 1 — Financial Sector Data Infrastructure:** Build high-quality financial-sector data infrastructure to help create reliable and trustworthy AI models.
2. **Recommendation 6 — Adaptive and Enabling Policies:** Create a clear, forward-looking AI policy framework based on the Seven Sutras, and regularly review existing policies to make sure they keep pace with AI risks and developments.
3. **Recommendation 8 — AI Liability Framework:** Create clear rules for who is responsible when AI causes harm, while encouraging organisations that put safety measures such as incident reporting, audits and red teaming in place.
4. **Recommendation 12 — Framework for Sharing Best Practices:** Create ways for financial institutions to share AI use cases, lessons learned and good governance practices so that responsible approaches can be adopted more widely.
5. **Recommendation 13 — Recognise and Reward Responsible AI Innovation:** Recognise and reward AI innovations that create positive social impact and build ethical considerations into their design.
6. **Recommendation 14 — Board Approved AI Policy:** Require each institution to have a Board-approved AI policy covering who is responsible, what risks are acceptable, what safeguards are required, how AI is reviewed, how customers are protected, and who is liable when things go wrong.
7. **Recommendation 15 — Data Lifecycle Governance:** Put clear controls around how data used by AI is collected, accessed, used, stored and deleted.
8. **Recommendation 16 — AI System Governance Framework:** Manage AI throughout its entire life—from design and development to deployment and retirement—with proper documentation, testing, monitoring and human oversight, particularly for higher-risk systems.
9. **Recommendation 17 — Product Approval Process:** Make sure AI-enabled products go through the organisation's normal approval process, with specific checks for AI-related risks before they are introduced.
10. **Recommendation 18 — Consumer Protection:** Protect customers through transparency, fairness and accessible ways to raise complaints or seek help, and educate them about safe AI use and their rights.
11. **Recommendation 19 — Cybersecurity Measures:** Identify security risks created by AI and strengthen the organisation's cybersecurity measures to address them.
12. **Recommendation 20 — Red Teaming:** Deliberately test AI systems to find weaknesses, harmful behaviour and ways they could be misused, and repeat these tests as risks change.
13. **Recommendation 24 — AI Audit Framework:** Regularly check whether AI systems are being governed properly, with independent audits for high-risk or complex systems.
14. **Recommendation 25 — Disclosures by REs:** Require institutions to disclose relevant information about their use of AI and establish a consistent approach to such disclosures.
15. **Recommendation 26 — AI Toolkit:** Develop a common toolkit that helps institutions check, compare and demonstrate whether they are following responsible AI principles such as fairness, transparency, accountability and robustness.

*Note: Recommendation numbers and titles follow the RBI FREE-AI Committee Report. The content under each Sutra is a focused extraction of the relevant part of the respective recommendation; the mapping of recommendations to the Seven Sutras is my interpretation.*
