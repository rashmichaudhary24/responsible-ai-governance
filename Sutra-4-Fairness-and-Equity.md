# Sutra 4: Fairness and Equity

## Why does it matter?

AI outcomes should be fair and non-discriminatory. In financial services, AI can influence who gets access to financial products and services, and it should not make existing exclusion or inequality worse.

AI should be designed and tested so that it does not unfairly disadvantage individuals or groups. It should also be used to improve access to financial services, particularly for people and communities that are underserved or excluded.

## What would this look like in practice?

RBI translates the Fairness and Equity Sutra into practical measures through:

1. **Recommendation 3 — Incentives and Funding Support:** Put incentives and infrastructure in place to encourage inclusive and equitable AI use among smaller entities.
2. **Recommendation 4 — Indigenous Financial Sector Specific AI Models:** Develop financial-sector-specific AI models, including LLMs, SLMs and non-LLM models, and make them available as a public good.
3. **Recommendation 5 — Integrating AI with DPI:** Create an enabling framework to integrate AI with Digital Public Infrastructure so that inclusive and affordable financial services can be delivered at scale.
4. **Recommendation 6 — Adaptive and Enabling Policies:** Create an AI policy framework for the financial sector, based on the Seven Sutras, to provide flexible guidance for innovation, adoption and risk management across the sector.
5. **Recommendation 7 — Enabling AI-Based Affirmative Action:** Encourage AI-driven innovation that helps improve financial inclusion for underserved and unserved sections of society, while keeping basic safeguards in place.
6. **Recommendation 13 — Recognise and Reward Responsible AI Innovation:** Recognise and reward responsible AI innovation, especially where it has positive social impact and is built with ethical considerations from the start.
7. **Recommendation 14 — Board Approved AI Policy:** Support smaller entities with an indicative policy template so that responsible AI governance is not limited to larger institutions.
8. **Recommendation 18 — Consumer Protection:** Protect customers through transparency, fairness and accessible recourse, and educate them about safe AI use and their rights.
9. **Recommendation 26 — AI Toolkit:** Develop a common toolkit that helps institutions check, compare and demonstrate compliance with responsible AI principles such as fairness, transparency, accountability and robustness.

*Note: Recommendation numbers and titles follow the RBI FREE-AI Committee Report. The content under this Sutra is a focused extraction of the relevant part of each recommendation; the mapping of recommendations to the Seven Sutras is my interpretation.*
